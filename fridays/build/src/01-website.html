Trùk 'i pan

A bread truck
that never
went home

Every island has a thing it does at midnight. Curacao grills.

Somebody cut a window in the side of a bread van and bolted a charcoal grill under it.

In the 1970s the bread trucks finished their rounds by morning and sat idle all night. Drivers started firing up charcoal in the back and selling to whoever was still out. That is a trùk 'i pan, literally a bread truck, and forty years later it is still how the island eats after dark.

The rules have not changed much. It opens when the restaurants close. Nothing is pre-cooked. You order at a window, you wait where you are standing, and the answer to every question is either batata or pan.

Fridays came late to that tradition, in 2018, and treats it seriously enough to put rib eye, picanha and conch on a paper plate at two in the morning.
