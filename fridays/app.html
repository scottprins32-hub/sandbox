Fridays currently rides a white-label of a shared island ordering platform. It works, but the brand is a skin on someone else's checkout and the app answers the wrong question. Nobody opens a food app at half past eleven to browse. They open it to find out how long, and when to leave the house.

So this one has no tab bar and no menu to browse. It is a single surface that changes identity through the night. Cold before nine. Quoting a live fire time once the coals are lit. And the moment you send an order it stops being a home screen and becomes the ticket, counting down to the minute you should walk out of the door.

The phone runs on Curaçao time, which never shifts for daylight saving. Left on Live it shows the truck as it is right now, which for most of the day is a cold grill, so the dial scrubs a night instead. Fire time, queue depth, the Trempan lane and the ticket all move with it.

The shape of it

Three decisions
the rest follows from

The ticket is the app

Not a menu, not a feed, not five tabs. Once you have ordered, the surface you were already looking at becomes a live ticket with a fire timeline and a collection code. Everything else is how you get to one.

Why it mattersCharcoal is not a microwave. If the wait is real, the honest move is to show it minute by minute rather than spin a progress bar.

It tells you when to leave

The app knows the depth of the rail and how far away you are. So it stops reporting status and starts giving an instruction: leave in six minutes and your food hits the window as you park. Change where you are coming from and the number moves.

Why it mattersFood that waits for a customer goes soft. A customer who waits at the window blocks the next twelve. Timing the handover fixes both.

It pays you to come early

Trempan is a lane, not a discount code. Book a collection slot in the first two hours after the grill lights, 21:00 to 23:00, and the kitchen guarantees twelve minutes and takes Cg 2 off, because an empty grill is worth more to them than a full one.

Why it mattersThe truck's only real problem is that everyone arrives between 22:30 and 01:00. Loyalty that moves demand beats loyalty that stamps a card.

ON THE SHARED PLATFORM

  • The menu arrives as a flat list, so the cut and format grid the kitchen actually runs on is flattened into fifty-nine near-duplicate lines.
  • No queue depth, so the quoted time is the same at 20:00 and at midnight.
  • The customer belongs to the platform. Fridays cannot message a regular or see who they are.
  • English and Dutch only, on an island whose first language is Papiamentu.
  • Commission on every order, on a Cg 21 plate.

ON THEIR OWN APP

  • The builder mirrors the window: pick the cut, pick the format, batata or pan, then sauce, with the cells the kitchen refuses saying so.
  • Fire time is quoted from the live rail, so the peak quotes the high thirties and the hour after lighting the mid teens.
  • Fridays keeps the relationship, the order history and the reason someone stopped coming.
  • Papiamentu first, with Dutch and English behind it. The website next door switches all three live.
  • Card fees only, and a cash-at-the-window option that costs nothing.

This prototype runs in English with the truck's own Papiamentu kept where the truck uses it: the greeting, the four questions, the words on the plate. The language switching itself is demonstrated on the website rather than duplicated here, because the Papiamentu still needs a native speaker's pass before any of it ships.
