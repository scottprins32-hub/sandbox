Synthetic · estimated · not athlete data

Geometry Lab

A four-camera rig is calibrated, used on a synthetic pitching session, made to fail in two realistic ways, diagnosed from its own residuals and judged with the validation script. Four global skills and one of mine ran together.

Calibration, judged on frames it never saw

Caliscope calibrated the rig from half of a ChArUco board sequence (5×7 squares of 0.150 m). The other half stayed out. aniposelib then triangulated those frames from the exported TOML.

The first board path was flat. Held-out points landed at 2.5 mm, yet Caliscope reported that intrinsic refinement was gated off for lack of depth range. The board now travels up to 1.8 m, tilts and turns, so the pitcher's height is covered.

Intrinsics are the synthetic ones and stayed fixed (the depth ratio is still 1.7 against Caliscope's gate of 2.0). A real rig needs a separate lens calibration, and a new calibration cannot recover the lens state of an old recording.

My first check selected the wrong columns of the truth table and still reported a good fit, because the board path was flat and a permuted axis fits a flat cloud. The cameras compared against truth exposed it. Named columns fixed it.

Two faults, two signatures

The session has detector noise (1.5 px), 8% dropouts and left/right swaps of hips and shoulders in about 1% of observations. On top of that: camera 3 is knocked by 1° after calibration (B), or camera 1 runs two frames late (C). For each camera the rig triangulates without it and measures how well the other three agree.

Dropping the right camera makes the rest agree. Residual against joint speed did not separate the two faults (0.70 and 0.67). The wrist-track lag from motion_validate.py did: 0 frames for B, +2 for C. B was repaired by excluding the camera, C by shifting its stream.

What the repair buys

Median wrist error at the release frame, over 30 synthetic pitchers.

aniposelib's RANSAC also brings the wrist down in both faults (12 and 9 mm) without diagnosing anything, but in B its angle then fails the ±5° test (bias +6.3°). Plain residual screening made C worse at the wrist (205 mm); I did not test why, and discarding the camera's good frames together with the late ones is only a guess.

  • The raw maximum is useless. One left/right swap in one frame sets the peak. ICC drops to 0.05 to 0.09 and the bias is +53° to +67°. A 5-frame median first brings it to ICC 0.94 on the clean session.
  • Equivalent on average is not equivalent per pitch. Scenario B after repair passes ±5° on the mean, with limits of agreement of −13.5° to +18.4° and ICC 0.65.
  • Fixing the camera made the angle look worse. Joint positions improved from 53 to 8 mm, while the repaired angle scatters more than the contaminated one (ICC 0.65 against 0.84). With three cameras the hips and shoulders are less constrained. That explanation was not tested separately. Which pipeline is better depends on whether the purpose is the angle or the release position.

Review and labels

FiftyOne (local, community): 40 frames of camera 0 and camera 3, with the reference, the detector and two predictions in separate fields. Failures were tagged: 5 missed joints, 18 geometry, 1 wrong anatomy, 16 ok. Three saved views and the dataset schema were exported, and re-importing the export reproduced all fields (passed). The app UI was not launched.

In camera 3's own image, the repaired 3D lands 25 px from the reference and the contaminated one 12 px. The repaired result is right in the world; the stale calibration places it wrongly in the image of the camera that moved. In camera 0 the repaired result is better (1.3 against 3.8 px). The image of the suspect camera is the wrong yardstick.

CVAT: a skeleton label spec with anatomical left and right, visibility codes 0/1/2 and immutable source frame ids, plus the reference as COCO keypoints. A write-and-read check of unique frame ids, valid codes, left/right names and preserved occluded and not-in-frame states: passed. No CVAT server exists here, so importing the files into one is untested, and so is interpolation. The reference is the synthetic truth, not human labels.

What this does not show

  • Nothing here is a real camera, lens or athlete. A zero-ish residual on synthetic data is software geometry only.
  • The classic aniposelib 0.8.0 ran. The PyTorch variant was not tested.
  • The detector is the truth plus noise, dropouts and swaps. A real pose model has correlated errors this does not have.
  • Timing was a whole-frame offset. Sub-frame offsets and drift were not simulated.
  • 30 pitchers that share one skeleton with scaled twist, lean and arm swing; the spread between them is invented.
